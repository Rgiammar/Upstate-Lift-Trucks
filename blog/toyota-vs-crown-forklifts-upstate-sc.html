Toyota vs Crown Forklifts:
Which Is Right for Your
Upstate SC Operation?

July 2026
8 min read
Greenville · Spartanburg · Anderson SC

Toyota and Crown are the two names that come up most often when Upstate SC operations are evaluating forklift brands. Both are genuinely excellent. Both have strong service coverage in the Greenville-Spartanburg market. And both are widely represented across the automotive manufacturing corridor, the distribution facilities near the SC Inland Port, and the warehouses along the I-85 and I-26 interchange. But they are built around different strengths, and choosing the wrong one for your specific application costs money over time.

Toyota Forklifts

Toyota Material Handling is the largest forklift manufacturer in the world by sales volume, a position it has held for over 15 consecutive years. In the Upstate SC market, Toyota's presence runs deep - particularly among the Tier 1 and Tier 2 automotive suppliers in Spartanburg and Anderson counties where propane-powered sit-down counterbalance units handle the mixed indoor and outdoor movement that automotive manufacturing demands.

What Toyota Does Best

  • Internal combustion forklifts. Toyota's propane, LP gas, and diesel-powered sit-down forklifts are among the most durable in the industry. For Upstate SC manufacturing operations that move between indoor staging areas and outdoor receiving yards, Toyota IC units are a standard choice for good reason.
  • Resale value. Toyota forklifts hold their value exceptionally well. A maintained used Toyota commands strong prices in the secondary market - meaningful for operations evaluating total cost of ownership over a 7 to 10 year horizon or planning to sell equipment when upgrading a fleet.
  • Parts and service availability. Toyota's dealer network is one of the most extensive in the industry. Parts availability is rarely an issue in the Greenville-Spartanburg market, and technicians familiar with Toyota equipment are widely available locally.
  • System of Active Stability (SAS). Toyota's proprietary stability system automatically monitors and adjusts the forklift's stability during operation. A meaningful safety advantage for operations handling uneven loads or working on imperfect surfaces, and a feature that resonates with the safety culture requirements of automotive customers conducting facility audits.
  • Capacity range. Toyota offers one of the widest capacity ranges in the industry, from compact 3,000 lb units to heavy-duty configurations well above 30,000 lbs, making it practical to standardize on one brand across multiple equipment types within a single facility.

Where Toyota Has Limitations

  • Toyota's electric counterbalanced forklifts are solid but not the standout product in their lineup. Crown and several other manufacturers lead on electric innovation, particularly in lithium-ion technology.
  • Toyota's narrow aisle equipment - reach trucks and order pickers - is competent but Crown is generally considered the benchmark in that category. For the distribution and 3PL operations near the SC Inland Port running high-bay racking in narrow aisles, this matters.
  • Some operators find Toyota cabins less ergonomically refined than Crown, which is a real factor in high-cycle operations where operators are in the seat for 8 or more hours per shift.

Crown Forklifts

Crown Equipment Corporation is one of the few major forklift manufacturers still family-owned, and all Crown equipment is manufactured in New Bremen, Ohio. Crown has built its reputation not on volume but on innovation - particularly in electric forklifts and warehouse equipment. In the Upstate SC distribution corridor along Woodruff Road and near the SC Inland Port in Greer, Crown electric equipment is increasingly the standard in facilities that run exclusively indoor electric fleets.

What Crown Does Best

  • Electric forklifts. Crown is widely considered the gold standard for electric counterbalanced forklifts. Their AC motor technology delivers strong performance and battery efficiency, and their lithium-ion offerings are among the most advanced available. For the growing number of Upstate SC distribution facilities requiring emission-free indoor operation, Crown is the most commonly specified brand.
  • Reach trucks and narrow aisle equipment. Crown's reach truck line is a benchmark product. Their ESR series units are known for lift height, operator visibility, and precision in tight aisle configurations - exactly what the high-bay 3PL operations near the Inland Port in Greer require.
  • Operator ergonomics. Crown invests heavily in operator comfort and fatigue reduction. Their cabins feature excellent sightlines, intuitive controls, and ergonomic design that reduces fatigue in high-cycle operations. For facilities running two or three shifts, this has a real impact on productivity and injury rates.
  • InfoLink fleet management. Crown's integrated fleet management system provides real-time data on equipment utilization, operator behavior, impact detection, and maintenance alerts. For multi-unit operations that need visibility into fleet performance, InfoLink is one of the more capable systems available at this price point.
  • Order pickers. Crown's order picker line is consistently rated among the best in the industry for e-commerce and retail distribution applications where case-picking speed and accuracy are critical.

Where Crown Has Limitations

  • Crown's internal combustion lineup is more limited than Toyota's. For Upstate SC manufacturing operations that need reliable propane or diesel-powered equipment for outdoor and mixed-use applications, Toyota is typically the stronger choice.
  • Crown parts and service, while excellent, can have slightly longer lead times in some situations than Toyota due to a somewhat smaller dealer footprint in certain areas. Service coverage in the Greenville-Spartanburg market is solid but worth confirming for your specific location before committing.
  • Crown equipment typically carries a higher upfront purchase price than comparable Toyota units, though total cost of ownership on their electric equipment often balances this out over time through lower maintenance costs and better battery life.

Which Is Right for Your Operation?

Choose Toyota if you...
  • Run propane, LP gas, or diesel forklifts
  • Need outdoor or mixed indoor/outdoor capability
  • Operate in automotive manufacturing or supplier environments
  • Value maximum parts and service availability
  • Are buying used equipment with resale in mind
  • Need a wide capacity range from one brand
  • Prioritize long-term proven reliability
Choose Crown if you...
  • Run primarily electric equipment indoors
  • Operate in narrow aisle or high-bay configurations
  • Need reach trucks or order pickers
  • Run distribution or 3PL operations near the Inland Port
  • Prioritize operator ergonomics in high-cycle operations
  • Want advanced fleet management and telematics
  • Are investing in lithium-ion battery technology

What Upstate SC Operations Actually Run

The split between Toyota and Crown in the Upstate SC market follows the market's two dominant facility types fairly cleanly.

Automotive Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers in Spartanburg and Anderson counties - operations feeding into BMW, Michelin, and the broader automotive supply chain - run heavily on Toyota propane. The mixed indoor/outdoor movement, multi-shift schedules, and the fast tank swap capability of propane make Toyota IC the practical standard in this segment. Several of these facilities have been on Toyota for decades and have no operational reason to change.

Distribution and 3PL facilities along the Woodruff Road corridor and near the SC Inland Port in Greer tell a different story. These are largely modern, enclosed, emission-sensitive environments running high-bay racking. Crown electric and reach trucks are common in this segment, particularly in facilities that supply into food, e-commerce, or consumer goods chains with indoor air quality requirements.

Mixed fleets are also common - and often the right answer. A facility that does receiving and yard work on propane Toyota units and runs Crown electric reach trucks inside the high-bay storage area is not an unusual configuration. The two brands complement each other well when the application is genuinely split between outdoor/IC work and indoor narrow-aisle work.

The Bottom Line

Toyota and Crown are both strong choices - the question is which one matches your specific application. Toyota wins on IC power, outdoor versatility, parts availability, and resale value. Crown wins on electric performance, narrow aisle and reach truck equipment, and operator ergonomics. For a significant portion of Upstate SC operations the real answer is a combination of both, with each brand covering the part of the operation it does best.

The best way to evaluate either brand is to get side-by-side quotes from local independent providers who carry both - and if possible, demo each in your actual facility before committing to a purchase or long-term lease. Equipment that performs well in a showroom does not always perform the same way in a specific facility layout at production volume.
